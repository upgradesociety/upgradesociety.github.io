## Part I — Foundations of the Libre Currency
### 1. Concept and Origins
- Ğ1 (“June”) as the first implementation of the *Théorie Relative de la Monnaie* (TRM) by Stéphane Laborde.  
- The principle of **freedom in money creation**: every individual co-creates money equally through time.  
- Comparison with Bitcoin and fiat systems:  
  - Bitcoin → free software, but asymmetrical issuance (machines, early adopters).  
  - Fiat → centrally controlled and debt-based.  
  - Ğ1 → symmetrical and human-based issuance.

### 2. The Universal Dividend (UD)
- Core unit of issuance: UD (Dividende Universel).  
- Calculated proportionally to the average monetary mass (M/N).  
- Issued regularly by every certified member.  
- Ensures both spatial and temporal symmetry in money creation.  
- The UD as a measure of value rather than a reserve asset.

### 3. Relative Money Theory (TRM)
- Money as a **relative measurement tool**, not an intrinsic store of value.  
- Inflation redefined: the perception of prices changes as reference frames evolve.  
- Importance of logarithmic scaling for human lifespan and economic equilibrium.  
- The TRM as both a mathematical and ethical framework for monetary fairness.

## Part II — Technical Architecture
### 4. Duniter Blockchain
- Open-source peer-to-peer protocol for decentralized issuance.  
- Core functions: block creation, member verification, UD generation.  
- Security through Web of Trust (WoT) — prevents multiple identities.  
- Evolution toward **Duniter V2**, focused on:
  - Improved scalability.  
  - New “forger” node structure.  
  - Modular network governance.

### 5. Wallets and Interfaces
- **Cesium** as the principal wallet and identity manager.  
- Mobile, web, and desktop implementations.  
- User roles:
  - *Co-creator wallets* (members generating UD).  
  - *Simple wallets* (non-members holding and transacting).  
- Integration with Cesium+ social layer (marketplaces, profiles, reputation).

### 6. Web of Trust and Identity Verification
- Each human must be certified by at least 5 existing members.  
- Certifications expire to ensure active community health.  
- Mechanism prevents Sybil attacks and maintains human uniqueness.  
- Discussion on forking possibilities: Ğ2, Ğ3 networks with independent WoTs.

## Part III — Economic Logic and Comparative Analysis
### 7. Inflation and Monetary Symmetry
- Ğ1 issuance does not produce inflation in TRM terms because all members’ balances grow proportionally.  
- Prices adjust relatively but purchasing power equality remains stable over long periods.  
- “Inflation” in fiat systems results from asymmetric issuance — not applicable to UD logic.  

### 8. Valuation and Mixed Pricing
- Theoretical debate about “mixed prices” combining Ğ1 and euros.  
- In TRM: currencies have distinct reference frames — non-convertible by formula.  
- Empirical data: real exchanges since 2017 show rates around **1 € = 3–5 DU**.  
- Mixed pricing seen as transitional rather than contradictory.  

### 9. Comparison with Other Systems
- **Bitcoin:** scarce, deflationary, machine-issued, speculative.  
- **LETS systems:** similar community logic but no universal issuance principle.  
- **Fiat systems:** debt-based and centralized issuance.  
- **Ğ1:** uniquely combines decentralization with human-centered issuance and measurable symmetry.

## Part IV — Markets, Exchange, and Real-World Practice
### 10. ĞMarkets: The Social Economy of Exchange
- Ğmarkets as local barter-like gatherings for exchange of goods, services, and culture.  
- Principle: “Economy is exchange; life is exchange.”  
- Exchanges often symbolic — food, arts, music, teaching, care services.  
- Examples from France, Belgium, Denmark, Ibiza, and Madeira.  
- Ğmarkets as spaces for community building and education.  

### 11. Circulation Challenges
- Limited availability of material goods (producers reluctant to accept Ğ1).  
- Excess of service offers and scarcity of essential goods.  
- Circularity problem: accumulation without outlets.  
- Solutions discussed:
  - Mixed (Ğ1 + €) pricing.  
  - Coordination across regions.  
  - Professional integration (farmers, artisans, health practitioners).

### 12. Paper Ğ1 and Symbolic Experiments
- Use of printed bills linked to wallets via QR codes.  
- Function: educational and onboarding tool for newcomers.  
- Trials in markets: distributing small amounts of Ğ1 in paper form.  
- Reinforces tangible connection and understanding of digital currency.

### 13. Digital Infrastructure for Exchange
- Platforms:  
  - **Ğchange** — marketplace for goods and services.  
  - **Girala** — decentralized directory for global use.  
  - **AirBnJune** — hospitality network for libre travelers.  
- Challenges in maintaining uptime, translations, and technical support.  
- API and integration ideas (Cesium-compatible commerce systems).

## Part VI — Future Directions and Theoretical Reflections
### 17. Scaling the Libre Economy
- Discussion on achieving greater economic diversity.  
- Need for producers, farmers, and SMEs to join the ecosystem.  
- Hybrid financial models combining Ğ1 and fiat for transition.  
- Incentives for real-world adoption (education, trust, and usability).

### 18. Open-Source Economics and Governance
- Ğ1 as an experiment in *open monetary governance*.  
- Decentralized verification parallels decentralized software collaboration.  
- Proposal for civic or municipal integrations.  
- Potential alignment with other open economies or crypto projects (Zcash, Monero, Nano).

### 19. The Philosophical Meaning of Free Money
- Money as a relative and cooperative human construct.  
- The TRM reframes monetary justice as mathematical symmetry.  
- The Universal Dividend as social recognition of human participation.  
- Libre currency as prototype for a sustainable, non-debt-based civilization.
