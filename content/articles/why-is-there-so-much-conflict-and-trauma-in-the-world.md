# Why Is There So Much Conflict and Trauma in the World? Why do We Feel So Bad?

*(And what Switzerland, fiat money, Bitcoin, and G1 reveal about the root causes)*

---

## 0. What This Video Is Really About

- This is not a political attack  
- This is not about blaming people  
- This is a structural and philosophical diagnosis  

**Core question:**

> Why do human societies — even rich, educated ones — keep producing conflict, resentment, burnout, and trauma?

---

## 1. Conflict Is Not the Cause — It’s the Symptom

### 1.1 Surface conflicts we focus on
- Political polarization  
- Culture wars  
- Class resentment  
- Identity conflicts  

These are **expressions**, not origins.

### 1.2 Trauma emerges from powerlessness
- Inability to say no  
- Inability to opt out  
- Inability to start over  
- Inability to influence decisions  

> Trauma is what happens when pressure meets powerlessness.

WHY?
- The 3 Base of the Pyramid (Autonomy, Competence, Belonging)
- Usually we want to achieve this but our inner world is not aligned with our outer world. In other words, the system creates the incentives for certain status in certain conditions, but we're really not being able to fulfill that. Especially because the system makes it so hard.
	- Many people are trapped to a job they don't like, a routine they dislike, etc


---

## 2. The Missing Philosophy: Voluntarism

### 2.1 What voluntarism means
- Human relations should be voluntary  
- Cooperation over coercion  
- Consent over obedience  
- Exit options over forced participation  

Historically present in:
- Guilds  
- Free cities  
- Swiss cantons  
- Mutual aid societies  

### 2.2 Europe’s historical inheritance
- Authority preceded consent  
- Monarchy → Church → State  
- Obedience normalized over centuries  

> Europe learned order before it learned freedom.

---

## 3. Modern Europe: Safety Without Agency

### 3.1 The paradox
- High taxes  
- High regulation  
- High protection  

But also:
- Difficult to start a business  
- Difficult to fail safely  
- Difficult to build independently  
- Difficult to opt out  

### 3.2 Psychological outcome
- People are not slaves  
- But they live **economically scripted lives**  
- Responsibility is individual  
- Control is centralized  

This mismatch produces conflict and trauma.

---

## 4. Fiat Money as a Trauma Engine

### 4.1 Structural properties of fiat money
- Centralized issuance  
- Political discretion  
- Inflation as invisible taxation  
- Permanent debt expansion  

### 4.2 Psychological consequences
- Constant anxiety  
- Erosion of savings  
- Short-term thinking  
- Social distrust  

> When money is unfair, society becomes psychologically unstable.

---

## 5. Why Luxury Economies Don’t Heal Trauma

### 5.1 Luxury as an escape hatch
- Capital concentration  
- Status signaling  
- Artificial scarcity  

Works for:
- A minority  

Fails for:
- Societies  
- Stability  
- Mental health  

### 5.2 The ethical dilemma
> A system that requires most people to lose so that a few can escape will always generate conflict.

---

## 6. Switzerland: A Living Counterexample

### 6.1 Structural differences
- Direct democracy  
- Strong local autonomy  
- Decentralized power  
- Frequent referendums  

### 6.2 Psychological effects
- People feel heard  
- Conflict is absorbed early  
- Responsibility matches power  

> Less trauma emerges when people are participants, not spectators.

---

## 7. Money Alternatives: Bitcoin and G1

### 7.1 Bitcoin — monetary voluntarism
- Opt-in money  
- No central issuer  
- No political control  
- Exit from fiat abuse  

> Bitcoin restores consent to money.

### 7.2 G1 (Relative Money / Ğ1)
- Equal monetary issuance  
- No debt-based creation  
- Monetary dignity by design  
- Long-term fairness  

Not a replacement — a **parallel layer**.

---

## 8. The Missing Layer: Parallel, Voluntary Systems

### 8.1 Why options reduce trauma
- Parallel money  
- Parallel economies  
- Parallel communities  
- Parallel governance  

Change through evolution, not revolution.

### 8.2 Ethical orientation
- Enter the system to survive  
- Build alternatives to heal  
- Do not become what you oppose  

> Ethics is not purity — it’s direction.

---

## 9. Redefining Winning: The Geist Problem

### 9.1 Why resentment grows
- Every historical period has a dominant **Geist**
- The Geist defines:
  - What is admired
  - What is rewarded
  - What “success” looks like
- People internalize this definition, even when it harms them

> Resentment grows when the world rewards a form of winning that most people cannot reach — and cannot opt out of.

### 9.2 Inner world vs outer world
- Inner world: values, dignity, desire for meaning  
- Outer world: incentives, rewards, visibility  

When the gap between the two becomes too large:
- Resentment appears  
- Trauma accumulates  
- Conflict externalizes  

---

## 10. A Map of Historical Geist Archetypes

### 10.1 Survival / Tribal Geist
**“We survive together or we die.”**
- Winning: strength, loyalty, protection  
- Authority: elders, warriors  
- Trauma: violence, constant threat  

### 10.2 Mythic–Religious Geist
**“The world has meaning because God(s) command it.”**
- Winning: obedience, purity  
- Authority: priests, divine kings  
- Trauma: guilt, repression  

### 10.3 Feudal–Honor Geist
**“Know your place and fulfill your duty.”**
- Winning: honor, title, lineage  
- Authority: nobility  
- Trauma: humiliation, powerlessness  

### 10.4 Mercantile–Early Liberal Geist
**“Skill, trade, and contracts create freedom.”**
- Winning: independence, mastery  
- Authority: guilds, cities  
- Trauma: risk, failure  

### 10.5 Industrial–National Geist
**“Progress through production and nation.”**
- Winning: productivity, discipline  
- Authority: nation-states  
- Trauma: alienation, war  

### 10.6 Technocratic–Bureaucratic Geist
**“Systems and experts know best.”**
- Winning: compliance, credentials  
- Authority: institutions  
- Trauma: learned helplessness  

### 10.7 Consumer–Status Geist
**“You are what you display.”**
- Winning: visibility, luxury  
- Authority: markets, media  
- Trauma: anxiety, envy  

### 10.8 Financialized–Fiat Geist
**“Money creates reality.”**
- Winning: asset ownership, leverage  
- Authority: central banks, finance  
- Trauma: insecurity, resentment  

### 10.9 Transitional Crisis Geist (Now)
**“The old rules no longer work.”**
- Winning: unclear, fragmented  
- Authority: disputed  
- Trauma: polarization, nihilism  

### 10.10 Emerging Voluntary Geist (Potential)
**“Meaning, money, and power must be consensual.”**
- Winning: autonomy, contribution, resilience  
- Authority: local, opt-in, transparent  
- Trauma: reduced and absorbed early  

> Geists do not disappear — they stack.  
> Progress comes from consciously transitioning, not destroying.

---

## 11. Conclusion: Changing the World by Redefining Winning

- We cannot heal trauma by force  
- We cannot end conflict by ideology  
- We transition forward by:
  - Becoming aware of the current Geist  
  - Redefining what it means to win  
  - Building ourselves for the next world  

> The future is not imposed.  
> It is grown — person by person, system by system.
