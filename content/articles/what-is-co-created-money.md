## Why Co-Created Money Has The Potential To Change The World
Universal Basic Income is now part of mainstream political discourse. Across the political spectrum, there is broad agreement that something like UBI will eventually be necessary: automation, AI, demographic decline, and job precarity are all pushing in the same direction. The disagreement is no longer whether UBI is needed, but how it should be implemented, funded, and governed.

What is almost never discussed is that there is another path entirely.

Not just a different welfare policy.  
Not just a different distribution mechanism.  
But a different kind of monetary network.

This is where **co-created money** becomes important.

Most UBI proposals still assume the same monetary architecture we already have. Money is created from above — by banks, central banks, states, or financial institutions — and then later redistributed downward through taxation, welfare, subsidies, or public transfers.

Co-created money starts somewhere else entirely.

It asks a more fundamental question:

**What if ordinary people did not merely receive money after the fact, but participated equally in creating it from the beginning?**

That is what makes co-created money more radical than UBI as it is usually imagined.

UBI usually means:
- the existing system remains in place,
- money is still issued hierarchically,
- and some portion of it is later redistributed to everyone.

Co-created money means:
- the monetary system itself changes,
- money is created from the bottom up,
- and each human becomes part of the issuance layer.

That is not just social policy.  
It is **monetary redesign**.

It means that instead of entering the economy only through wages, debt, state transfers, or capital ownership, a person enters it as a legitimate co-creator of the currency itself.

This changes the meaning of income.

Under normal UBI, income is usually framed as support.  
Under co-created money, income is better understood as **participation**.

It is not charity.  
It is not redistribution.  
It is not a concession from power.  

It is membership in a living economic network.

This is why co-created money can be thought of as a new kind of monetary commons. It does not merely move value around inside the old system. It creates a new layer of exchange, a new logic of entry, and potentially a new foundation for local and regional economies.

And this matters especially in places where there is little circulating money.

Many towns and regions do not only suffer from lack of productivity or lack of people. They also suffer from lack of **monetary circulation**. Skills exist. Time exists. Land exists. Empty buildings exist. Needs exist. But exchange remains weak because the money network is thin, absent, or controlled from elsewhere.

A co-created monetary network can partially solve that by introducing purchasing power directly into the community, without waiting first for banks, investors, subsidies, or state intervention.

## Why AI Makes This Even More Fundamental

This becomes even more important in a world shaped by AI.

As AI becomes more capable, a growing share of economically valuable tasks will be automated, centralized, and concentrated in the hands of those who own the models, the infrastructure, the data, and the capital. Productivity may explode, but that does not automatically mean purchasing power will be distributed more fairly.

In fact, the opposite may happen.

The current system gives those closest to capital and technological leverage the strongest structural advantage. If AI massively increases productive capacity while ownership remains concentrated, then more wealth may flow upward while more people become economically marginal to the existing labor market.

This is exactly why co-created money matters.

If the future contains:
- less need for human labor,
- more concentration of productive power,
- and more dependence on systems owned by a small number of actors,

then relying on the same actors to voluntarily fund or sustain a large-scale UBI may be unrealistic.

There is no guarantee that the wealthy, large corporations, or the present institutional order will willingly create a durable, unconditional, non-coercive income floor for everyone. Even if they discuss UBI, they may prefer limited, conditional, bureaucratic, or politically controlled versions of it.

That is why a co-created monetary network is so fundamental.

It does not wait for permission from those who already dominate capital.  
It does not depend on billionaire philanthropy.  
It does not rely on the current winners of the AI economy deciding to share power.

It creates a different base layer altogether.

In an AI-heavy future, this may become one of the most important distinctions of all:

**Will people only receive access to economic life through institutions above them, or will they retain a direct share in monetary creation itself?**

This is also why Ğ1 is interesting.

Ğ1 is not just “an alternative currency.” It is an early working example of what a post-labor, post-scarcity-oriented, human-centered monetary network could begin to look like. It offers a way of thinking about economic participation that does not depend entirely on jobs, state redistribution, or the goodwill of concentrated power.

Only after understanding this difference does Ğ1 become fully interesting.

Because Ğ1 is not simply “a UBI coin.”

It is an actual example of co-created money already functioning in the real world.

## Ğ1: A Live Example of Co-Created Money

Ğ1 (pronounced June) is not a proposal, not a pilot program, and not a government experiment. It is a live monetary system where every participant creates money regularly, simply by being a human member of the network.

In other words, Ğ1 is **co-created money in practice**.

The idea is simple but profound: money does not come from banks, the state, miners, or privileged early actors. It comes from human beings themselves, equally, through a shared monetary rule.

This income is called the **Universal Dividend (UD)**, and it is issued equally to all certified members for life.

In practical terms, Ğ1 implements one of the most radical forms of UBI imaginable:
a UBI with no state, no taxes, no debt, no central authority, and no redistribution.

## 1. The Core Principle: Human-Based Money Creation

All monetary systems differ primarily in one thing: who is allowed to create money.

In fiat systems, money is created by banks and states through credit and monetary policy. Individuals enter the system through debt, wages, or transfers. The power to issue money is centralized and political.

In Bitcoin, money is created by machines. Issuance is algorithmic, fixed, and asymmetrical across time. Early participants benefit structurally from scarcity and technological advantage.

In Ğ1, money is created by humans themselves. Each person receives the same relative share of new money, continuously, across their lifetime.

This is what **co-created money** means in the strongest sense: not merely a community currency, not local barter, and not redistribution after the fact, but a monetary system in which every person is an equal issuer of new money. The source of money creation is no longer institutions above society, but society itself.

This is not equality in absolute units, but equality in monetary power over time. The system is designed so that no generation, class, or technical elite can structurally dominate money creation.

This principle comes from Relative Money Theory (TRM), written by Stéphane Laborde in 2010, which states that money should be symmetrical across:

- space (between individuals), and
- time (between generations).

## 2. The Universal Dividend (UD)

The Universal Dividend is the heart of Ğ1.

Every certified member receives a daily UD. This is not funded by taxes or redistribution; it is newly created money. Everyone co-creates the same proportion of the total monetary mass.

This means:

- No one must be productive to receive it.
- No one can be excluded once a member.
- No one controls its issuance.

Currently the dividend is:

- ~10 Ğ1 per day
- ~300 Ğ1 per month
- ~3,650 Ğ1 per year

The UD is not meant to guarantee wealth or comfort. It guarantees monetary existence. It ensures that every human has permanent access to the economic system, independent of employment, capital, or political status.

In that sense, it is closer to **monetary citizenship** than welfare.

And this is exactly where co-created money matters: it does not wait for institutions to redistribute value downward. It starts by giving every person direct participation in monetary creation itself.

## 3. Voluntary UBI vs Political UBI

Most UBI proposals are political by nature. They depend on:

- taxation,
- budget negotiations,
- ideological battles,
- and state legitimacy.

They are vulnerable to:

- inflationary financing,
- populist manipulation,
- electoral reversal,
- and bureaucratic expansion.

Ğ1 bypasses all of this. It does not require political power. It requires only collective agreement.

Participation is voluntary. Exchange is voluntary. No one is forced to accept the currency. There is no obligation, no coercion, no monopoly.

This is why it can be described as **voluntary UBI**: income generated by mutual consent, not imposed authority.

And more precisely, it is voluntary UBI because it is built on co-created money. The income does not come from taxing one group to pay another. It comes from a monetary rule that recognizes every member as equally entitled to participate in issuance.

## 4. Real Economy, Not Theory

Ğ1 is already used in practice.

Across France, Spain, and Portugal, there are regular markets and exchanges where people use Ğ1 to:

- sell food,
- rent accommodation,
- offer services,
- organize events,
- fund projects,
- and build local economic networks.

There are farmers producing food for Ğ1. There are hospitality and accommodation offers, including AirBnB-style options paid in Ğ1. There are local exchanges, meetups, and parallel circuits of trade that continue to grow.

This is not a simulation. It is a parallel economy operating in real time, outside state monetary systems.

That is what makes co-created money so important: it is not just a theory about fairness. It is already functioning as an economic layer that people can use to transact, cooperate, and build local circulation.

## 5. Why This Is Largely Invisible

Ğ1 does not fit existing ideological categories.

It is not socialist, because there is no state.  
It is not capitalist, because capital accumulation gives no structural advantage in money creation.  
It is not libertarian in the usual sense, because issuance is collective and human-based.  
It is not crypto-speculative, because there is no scarcity narrative.

It removes both profit extraction and political control from money issuance. As a result, it generates little interest from institutions, media, or financial actors. There is no obvious power center to promote it, regulate it, or attack it.

Its logic is simple:

**money as a commons, not as a weapon.**

That may be the clearest way to understand co-created money. It treats money not as a scarce commodity distributed from above, but as a shared social infrastructure generated by equals.

## 6. The Deeper Implication

The most important lesson of Ğ1 is not technical. It is philosophical.

It demonstrates that UBI does not fundamentally require:

- governments,
- billionaires,
- taxation,
- or centralized planning.

It requires only coordination.

People agreeing that:

- money should not precede humans,
- no one should start life in monetary exclusion,
- and economic participation should not depend on power.

This is a radical shift in how we think about income, work, and value. It reframes UBI not as a political concession, but as a monetary right emerging from mutual recognition.

And that is the deeper meaning of co-created money: it relocates the origin of money from institutions to persons, from hierarchy to reciprocity, from privilege to symmetry.

## Conclusion

Before we spend decades redesigning state-based UBI systems, it is worth acknowledging something uncomfortable:

The first fully functional UBI already exists.  
It is global, voluntary, non-coercive, and operational.

More than that, it is based on a principle that most political systems still avoid entirely:

**that money itself can be co-created by people equally, instead of issued from above.**

We just do not notice it because it does not ask permission from power.
