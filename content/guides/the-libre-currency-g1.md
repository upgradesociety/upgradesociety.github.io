## Section 1 — Concept and Origins

  

### Historical Background

  

For thousands of years, human civilization has changed the way it represents value.

First, it was gold and silver — physical stores of trust.

Then came paper money, issued by kings and later by central banks.

In the twentieth century, we entered the fiat era — a system of promises without backing, based entirely on state authority.

And finally, in the twenty-first century, came digital money — numbers on screens, controlled by networks of banks and algorithms.

  

But all these systems share something in common: they are based on **asymmetry**.

Some issue the money, and others are forced to use it.

Some benefit from early access, while others chase rising prices.

In every case, monetary creation has remained a privilege, not a right.

  

In the early 2000s, a French engineer named Stéphane Laborde began asking a radical question.

What if money itself could be redesigned as a **free and equal human process**?

What if every individual could co-create money, not as a favor from the state, but as a birthright — simply by being alive and part of a community?

  

Laborde’s background was the open-source software world.

He was part of the same cultural ecosystem that gave us GNU, Linux, and Creative Commons — movements that replaced control with collaboration.

He realized that money could be understood in the same way as software: as a **shared human protocol** rather than a top-down decree.

  

This idea became known as the **Théorie Relative de la Monnaie**, or TRM — the *Relative Theory of Money*.

It was the first attempt to describe monetary justice not politically, but mathematically.

And from this theory, a new kind of currency was born: Ğ1, also called “June.”

  

---

  

### The Birth of Ğ1

  

Between 2010 and 2017, a small group of developers and thinkers gathered around Laborde to transform theory into practice.

They built **Duniter**, a blockchain designed not for speculation, but for human equality.

In March 2017, the first libre currency — Ğ1 — went live.

  

At the start, it was just a few hundred members from cities like Toulouse, Montpellier, Lyon, and Nantes.

They called it “June,” written with the letter Ğ and the number one — symbolizing the first of its kind.

Their goal was simple but profound: to test whether a community could maintain a stable economy where **every member creates money equally through time**.

  

It wasn’t about profit.

It was about **freedom** — the freedom to define value together, without permission.

And in that sense, Ğ1 became a living social experiment: part mathematics, part philosophy, part cooperative practice.

  

---

  

### Comparison Across Monetary Paradigms

  

To understand what makes Ğ1 different, let’s compare it with the two dominant monetary paradigms of our time — **Bitcoin** and **Fiat**.

  

Bitcoin is open-source and decentralized, but its issuance is not human-based.

It depends on computing power and energy.

Those with machines — and those who came early — hold an asymmetric advantage.

It is free software, yes, but not free money in the human sense.

  

Fiat currency, on the other hand, is created by central banks and lent into existence through debt.

It is neither transparent nor equal.

It rewards proximity to power and punishes those who live by labor alone.

Its supply expands for the few, while the many bear the cost through inflation.

  

Ğ1 introduces a third model — **human-based issuance**.

Instead of trusting machines or institutions, it trusts human equality itself.

Every certified member co-creates the same amount of new money, called the Universal Dividend.

There are no miners, no central authorities, no hidden privileges.

  

Governance is distributed.

Issuance is symmetrical.

And the ethical foundation is clear:

Freedom, equality, and transparency in the creation of value.

  

---

  

### Closing of Section 1

  

In just a few years, Ğ1 has grown into one of the most original monetary experiments in the world.

It is still small, still evolving, but its significance is deep.

It proposes not just a new currency, but a new definition of what money *is* — a shared human rhythm, not a tool of control.

  

In the next section, we’ll explore the core mechanism that makes this possible: the **Universal Dividend**, the heartbeat of the libre currency.

## Section 2 — The Universal Dividend (UD)

  

### Introduction

  

Every monetary system has a rhythm — a way new money enters circulation.

In fiat systems, this rhythm is dictated by central banks and interest rates.

In Bitcoin, it’s coded into the blockchain through mining rewards that halve every few years.

But in Ğ1, the rhythm is biological, not mechanical.

It follows the simple, universal passage of time — the same for everyone.

  

This rhythm is called the **Universal Dividend**, or UD.

It is the heartbeat of the libre currency.

  

---

  

### What is the Universal Dividend?

  

In Ğ1, every certified member of the community co-creates the same amount of money at regular intervals.

No exceptions.

It doesn’t matter how rich, poor, skilled, or connected you are.

Every human is equal before money creation.

  

The formula is simple:

UD = c × (M / N).

  

That means the dividend is proportional to the average money supply — M — divided by the number of members — N — multiplied by a constant rate — c — usually set around ten percent per year.

  

This ensures that the money supply grows organically and symmetrically, keeping pace with the number of people in the system and the passing of time.

  

---

  

### The Logic of Symmetry

  

Most systems of money creation are asymmetric.

They privilege those who come first — miners in Bitcoin, bankers in fiat.

They reward those who are already inside the system.

  

But the Universal Dividend introduces **temporal and spatial symmetry**.

  

Spatial symmetry means that every living member of the community creates the same share of money.

No one is excluded from creation.

It’s not charity; it’s participation.

  

Temporal symmetry means that every generation creates its own share of money, relative to its lifespan.

Young members start smaller, but as they keep co-creating, they converge toward the same average wealth as older members.

Over a lifetime, everyone approaches equilibrium.

  

This simple mechanism prevents the concentration of wealth at the top and the perpetual inequality of early access.

It builds fairness directly into the fabric of money itself.

  

---

  

### A Measure of Value, Not a Store of Power

  

The Universal Dividend redefines what money is meant to be.

In the TRM, money is not a store of power or a speculative asset.

It is a **measure of economic space and time**, a tool for coordination between equals.

  

Every day, every week, new money is born from human existence itself — not from debt, not from machines, not from privilege.

It’s a living measurement of participation, not domination.

  

In that sense, Ğ1 is closer to a clock than to a vault.

It measures human activity in equal intervals, instead of concentrating control in a few hands.

It reminds us that value is relative, shared, and constantly renewed — just like life.

  

---

  

### Economic Implications

  

Because everyone’s balance increases proportionally, Ğ1 is not inflationary in the traditional sense.

Yes, the total supply grows, but everyone’s share grows with it.

Prices adjust naturally, and purchasing power equality remains stable over the long term.

  

There are no interest rates, no quantitative easing, no banking hierarchies.

The economy becomes self-balancing — not by decree, but by design.

  

And perhaps most importantly, the UD acts as a form of **economic floor** — a basic income embedded in the structure of money itself.

No one has to apply for it or beg for it.

It simply flows, like sunlight, to every living member of the network.

  

---

  

### Ethical and Philosophical Meaning

  

At its core, the Universal Dividend is not just an economic formula.

It’s a moral statement.

It declares that existence alone is enough to justify participation in the economy.

  

It recognizes human life as the fundamental source of value — before productivity, before property, before politics.

  

Every person, by living and exchanging, becomes a co-creator of the shared world.

And that’s what makes the libre currency radically different from any monetary system that came before.

  

---

  

### Transition

  

In this framework, money ceases to be an instrument of power and becomes a common language of equality.

But how is this system maintained in practice?

How does the network ensure that only real people co-create, and that no one cheats?

  

To understand that, we need to look deeper — into the architecture that keeps the libre currency alive.

## Section 3 — The Relative Theory of Money (TRM)

  

### Introduction

  

Behind the libre currency, there is a deep philosophical and mathematical foundation.

It’s called the **Théorie Relative de la Monnaie**, or the Relative Theory of Money.

It is not a political theory or an ideology.

It’s a framework that tries to describe, in the most neutral and scientific way possible, what a fair monetary system should look like — one that treats every human being as an equal in space and time.

  

The TRM is the blueprint that gave birth to Ğ1.

It defines why the Universal Dividend exists, how it should evolve, and what principles guarantee long-term fairness.

  

---

  

### Money as a Relative Tool

  

In traditional economics, money is treated as a thing — as if it were gold, or oil, or some commodity that stores value.

But in the TRM, money is not a thing.

It is a **measurement system** — a relative tool that allows individuals to compare value across space and time.

  

Just as we use meters to measure distance and seconds to measure time, we use money to measure economic interaction.

It is not supposed to favor one group over another.

It is supposed to serve as a neutral reference frame between human beings.

  

This is why the TRM rejects the idea of “absolute value.”

There is no fixed price for a good, just as there is no absolute position in space.

Everything depends on the observer’s perspective — on time, culture, and the flow of exchange.

Money must therefore be *relative*, not absolute.

  

---

  

### The Logarithmic Principle

  

To ensure fairness across generations, the TRM uses a logarithmic model.

Laborde observed that human life expectancy sets the natural boundary for how long one can participate in economic creation.

So, he asked a simple question: how can money remain fair across different lifespans?

  

The answer lies in using logarithmic scaling.

Instead of issuing a fixed amount forever, the system grows proportionally with the monetary mass.

That means each generation receives an equal share of creation relative to its time on Earth.

Young people start with little, but over decades, they approach the same average wealth as those before them.

  

In mathematical terms, this creates equilibrium.

In ethical terms, it creates justice.

  

It means your economic dignity is not tied to when you were born, or how early you joined the system.

Time itself becomes the great equalizer.

  

---

  

### Ethical Interpretation

  

The TRM transforms monetary fairness from a political question into a measurable, mathematical one.

It says: freedom is not just a slogan; it’s a structure.

  

If money creation is asymmetric — controlled by a few, or concentrated in the past — then freedom is an illusion.

But if everyone co-creates money equally, freedom becomes a daily, concrete reality.

  

This is why the libre currency calls itself *human-centered*.

It’s not based on machines, governments, or corporations.

It’s based on human equality — quantified, encoded, and renewed every day through the Universal Dividend.

  

---

  

### A New Economic Philosophy

  

The TRM leads to a profound redefinition of economics itself.

Instead of asking, “How do we accumulate capital?” it asks, “How do we distribute creation fairly?”

Instead of “Who owns the money?” it asks, “Who creates it?”

  

Ownership and control are replaced by participation and flow.

Money becomes less about storing value and more about maintaining balance.

It becomes a living system, just like an ecosystem — dynamic, self-regulating, and adaptive.

  

This perspective aligns economics with the laws of life.

In nature, nothing is static, nothing is hoarded forever.

Things circulate, regenerate, and evolve.

The same principle, applied to money, produces a sustainable civilization.

  

---

  

### Closing of Section 3

  

The Relative Theory of Money unites mathematics and ethics in one simple idea:

that every human being, in every generation, deserves equal access to the means of exchange.

  

It doesn’t promise wealth; it promises fairness.

It doesn’t centralize power; it distributes creation.

And from that foundation, everything else — from technology to culture — can evolve freely.

## Section 4 — The Duniter Blockchain

  

### Introduction

  

So far, we’ve explored the theory — the moral and mathematical foundations of the libre currency.

But how do you turn such a theory into something practical?

How do you make sure that every human co-creates money fairly, without central control or corruption?

  

That’s where **Duniter** comes in.

Duniter is the technological backbone of Ğ1 — the open-source blockchain designed specifically for human-based monetary creation.

  

---

  

### A Blockchain with a Human Soul

  

When most people hear the word “blockchain,” they think of Bitcoin or Ethereum — networks powered by machines competing for computational dominance.

They imagine energy-intensive mining farms and speculative trading.

  

Duniter is completely different.

It’s not about machines.

It’s about **people**.

  

Its design doesn’t rely on proof of work or proof of stake.

Instead, it runs on **proof of personhood** — the idea that each participant in the network represents a real, unique human being.

Every certified member can participate in maintaining the network by running a node, forging blocks, and verifying transactions.

  

No one earns special privileges for running hardware.

There are no miners, no hidden elites — only equal participants, co-creating both the money and the trust that sustains it.

  

---

  

### Core Architecture

  

The Duniter network operates as a peer-to-peer system.

Each node maintains the full ledger — the history of all Universal Dividends and transactions.

Blocks are created approximately every five minutes, each containing the new UDs generated by members and all the transactions since the last block.

  

Unlike Bitcoin, where block creation is a race, Duniter randomly selects members who meet certain participation criteria to create the next block.

This randomness preserves fairness while keeping the process lightweight and energy-efficient.

  

The protocol is completely open-source, meaning anyone can read, modify, or fork it.

This transparency ensures that no single group can control the monetary rules.

If a community disagrees, it can always start its own branch — its own version of the libre currency.

  

---

  

### The Web of Trust

  

To prevent abuse — like someone pretending to be multiple people to create more money — Duniter uses a system called the **Web of Trust**.

Instead of relying on passwords or biometrics, it relies on **human relationships**.

Each new member must be certified by at least five existing members who personally verify their identity.

These certifications create a network of trust, where each node is a person and each connection represents real social recognition.

  

Certifications expire every two years, ensuring that the network remains alive and authentic.

Inactive members naturally fall off, and new participants take their place.

This constant renewal keeps the currency human — not frozen in a database, but evolving with its people.

  

---

  

### Duniter V2 — Evolution of the Network

  

The first version of Duniter was written in JavaScript and ran on modest hardware.

It proved the concept — but as the community grew, scalability became an issue.

  

That’s why developers have been working on **Duniter V2**, a complete rewrite in the Rust programming language.

V2 introduces a modular design, where nodes can specialize:

some forge blocks, some store data, and others serve as gateways for mobile wallets.

  

It also aims to integrate better interoperability — allowing multiple libre currencies, like potential “Ğ2” or “Ğ3,” to exist side by side, each with its own Web of Trust but shared technical foundations.

  

This is more than a blockchain upgrade — it’s the blueprint for a **federation of human economies**.

  

---

  

### Governance and Consensus

  

Duniter’s governance mirrors open-source software communities.

There are no CEOs or central authorities.

Decisions about upgrades and parameters are made through discussion, consensus, and voluntary participation.

  

If a majority of members disagree with a direction, they can fork the chain — creating a new path.

This means freedom isn’t just theoretical.

It’s embedded in the network’s DNA.

You can leave, create, or join as you wish — just like in a truly democratic society.

  

---

  

### The Ethical Dimension of Technology

  

Technology, in the Duniter vision, is not neutral.

It’s a reflection of our values.

By coding fairness and equality directly into the protocol, the developers turned the blockchain into an **ethical machine**.

It doesn’t just record transactions; it encodes a vision of human dignity.

  

When you use Ğ1, you’re not just sending money.

You’re participating in a social contract — one that says everyone matters equally, and that trust can be generated not by force, but by community.

  

---

  

### Closing of Section 4

  

Duniter is the invisible infrastructure that makes the libre currency possible.

It combines cryptography, social trust, and open-source governance into one living system.

It’s proof that technology can serve humanity — not the other way around.

---

Cesium is more than just a wallet.

Through Cesium, you can:

When you open Cesium, you’re not entering a corporate app; you’re entering a living ecosystem maintained by the people themselves.

  

---

  

### Co-Creator Wallets and Simple Wallets

  

In the libre currency, there are two kinds of wallets.

  

**Co-creator wallets** belong to certified members of the Web of Trust.

These wallets automatically generate the Universal Dividend each period.

They are living wallets — sources of monetary creation.

  

**Simple wallets** are for everyone else — users, businesses, or visitors who want to hold or transact without being certified.

They do not generate new money, but they are fully capable of trading and participating in the network.

  

This dual structure ensures openness without compromising fairness.

Anyone can join the economy, and those who want to co-create must simply verify their human uniqueness.

Just peer-to-peer cooperation.

Cesium was designed for inclusivity.

Its simplicity hides a deep philosophical truth:

If freedom is to be real, it must also be usable.

## Section 6 — Web of Trust and Identity Verification

  

### Introduction

  

Every digital system faces one central challenge — how to make sure that the people using it are real.

In traditional finance, this problem is solved through central control.

Banks ask for identification, governments issue documents, and corporations track behavior.

But in a libre economy, there is no central authority.

So how can we verify that each participant is a real human being, without surrendering freedom or privacy?

  

The answer is the **Web of Trust** — the human heart of the Ğ1 network.

  

---

  

### How the Web of Trust Works

  

The Web of Trust, often called the “WoT,” is a decentralized system of identity verification based on relationships, not bureaucracy.

When a new person wants to join the libre currency and become a co-creator, they must be **certified** by at least five existing members.

These certifications are like signatures of trust.

  

Each certifier confirms that they personally know this person and that the identity is unique — one human, one account.

Once the minimum number of certifications is reached, the new member begins co-creating the Universal Dividend.

  

This simple mechanism replaces government-issued IDs with community-issued recognition.

It’s a living social contract — built on familiarity, not control.

  

---

  

### A Dynamic, Self-Correcting Network

  

Certifications in the Web of Trust are not permanent.

They expire after two years.

This ensures that the network remains active, honest, and up-to-date.

  

Inactive or forgotten accounts naturally fade away, making space for new members.

Meanwhile, the rule of distance between members guarantees that the network remains well-connected.

Every participant must be reachable within a few degrees of separation from every other, maintaining the integrity of the human graph.

  

The system continuously renews itself, like a living organism that sheds what is no longer active.

It evolves with the people, never static, never locked by design.

  

---

  

### Security Against Abuse

  

One might ask, “What prevents someone from creating multiple identities?”

The Web of Trust itself provides the answer.

  

Because each certification comes from real relationships, creating multiple fake identities would require convincing many different members to certify someone who doesn’t exist.

That kind of manipulation is extremely difficult and becomes even harder as communities grow and cross-verify one another.

  

Moreover, every certification is public and traceable on the blockchain.

Anyone can see who certified whom, making dishonesty visible to all.

In this system, transparency replaces policing.

Trust is maintained not through surveillance, but through openness.

  

---

  

### A Human Approach to Identity

  

The Web of Trust redefines digital identity.

Instead of proving yourself to a machine or an authority, you prove yourself through human relationships.

Your credibility is not a piece of paper; it’s the network of people who know you, vouch for you, and interact with you.

  

It’s a return to the oldest form of social verification — community recognition — but expressed through modern technology.

  

This approach preserves both freedom and accountability.

It acknowledges that humans are social beings who validate one another through lived experience, not algorithms.

  

---

  

### Forking and Replication

  

Because the Duniter system is open-source, new communities can create their own Webs of Trust at any time.

Each new network can define its own certification rules — its own rhythm of trust.

We could one day see a family of libre currencies: Ğ1 in Europe, Ğ2 in Africa, Ğ3 in Latin America — each connected by philosophy but independent in structure.

  

Every Web of Trust would remain sovereign, yet compatible with others through shared technological standards.

This opens the door to a future of **federated human economies** — decentralized, interlinked, and self-governed.

  

---

  

### Philosophical Implications

  

The Web of Trust represents more than just a security model.

It’s a new way of imagining community in the digital age.

It replaces the culture of surveillance with a culture of relationship.

It transforms verification from control into cooperation.

  

In a world where identity has been turned into data, the WoT restores something sacred — the human face behind every transaction.

  

---

  

### Closing of Section 6

  

The Web of Trust ensures that the libre currency remains truly human.

It guarantees that money creation stays anchored to real lives, not to machines or institutions.

It is a technical system, yes, but also a social revolution — proof that networks of trust can replace networks of control.

## Section 7 — Inflation and Monetary Symmetry

  

### Introduction

  

Whenever a new currency is mentioned, one question always comes up:

“What about inflation?”

  

People have been taught to fear inflation — to see it as the great destroyer of savings and the ultimate sign of economic decay.

But in the libre currency, inflation doesn’t mean what we think it means.

In fact, it’s redefined completely.

  

To understand why, we need to look at how Ğ1 grows, and why this growth doesn’t distort the balance between people.

  

---

  

### The Nature of Inflation in Fiat Systems

  

In the fiat world, inflation happens because new money is created asymmetrically.

It’s issued first to banks, corporations, and governments — the so-called “early receivers.”

By the time it reaches ordinary citizens, prices have already risen.

That’s why the poor pay the inflation tax, while the rich often profit from it.

  

In such a system, money creation redistributes wealth from the bottom to the top.

It’s not just an economic phenomenon — it’s a political one.

Inflation becomes a weapon that preserves power.

  

---

  

### Inflation Redefined in Ğ1

  

The libre currency breaks that logic.

Because every member creates the same amount of new money, the overall supply grows evenly.

No one gets privileged access.

Everyone’s account expands at the same rhythm.

  

Yes, the total amount of money in circulation increases, but so does everyone’s balance.

Proportionally, the relationship between people remains stable.

In other words, your share of the economy doesn’t shrink just because new money is being created.

  

That’s what we mean by **monetary symmetry** — growth that maintains equality rather than eroding it.

  

---

  

### A Mathematical Perspective

  

The growth rate of Ğ1 is encoded in the Universal Dividend.

It’s set at a constant percentage of the total money supply — usually around ten percent per year.

This keeps the monetary system expanding at a logarithmic pace, matching the rate at which human generations change.

  

Over decades, everyone converges toward the same relative wealth, measured in units of UD.

Younger generations catch up naturally with older ones, because the money supply renews itself over time.

This is the temporal balance at the heart of the Relative Theory of Money.

  

When viewed through this lens, inflation is no longer a threat — it’s simply the reflection of life continuing.

It’s the rhythm of renewal, not the theft of value.

  

---

  

### Real-World Implications

  

In practice, Ğ1’s steady growth makes it a calm, predictable system.

There are no shocks, no speculative bursts, no central interventions.

The value of goods may shift relative to people’s choices and local conditions, but purchasing power equality stays stable.

  

Simulations and community data since 2017 show that even as the total amount of Ğ1 increases, the average ratio between members remains constant.

Everyone’s share evolves together.

There is no invisible hand taking from one to give to another.

  

This is the core difference between fiat inflation and what we might call *harmonic growth*.

The libre currency grows like an expanding spiral — not by accumulation, but by balance.

  

---

  

### A Cultural Shift in Understanding Value

  

To grasp this, we also need to rethink how we view money itself.

In the fiat world, money is seen as a fixed thing that must retain value over time.

In the libre world, money is a moving measure — a living reference frame.

  

Value doesn’t live in the currency; it lives in the people who exchange it.

What matters is not how much one unit can buy after ten years, but whether the community remains fair and free across generations.

  

This is a profound cultural shift — one that replaces anxiety with trust, and fear of loss with confidence in continuity.

  

---

  

### Beyond the Myth of Scarcity

  

Our current monetary systems are built on the myth of scarcity — the idea that there isn’t enough for everyone, that competition is necessary for survival.

But in truth, scarcity is often artificial.

It’s designed through monetary asymmetry and debt creation.

  

In Ğ1, abundance isn’t a dream.

It’s a mathematical structure.

When everyone co-creates money, scarcity loses its power as a political tool.

Economies can once again reflect human creativity, not institutional fear.

  

---
