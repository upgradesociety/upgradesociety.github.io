Ğ1 (“June”) is the first real-world currency built on the **Relative Theory of Money (TRM)**, developed by Stéphane Laborde.

The core idea is surprisingly simple:

>*Every human should create money equally, over time.*

In Ğ1, money creation does not depend on having a job, owning capital, taking loans, or being approved by banks or the state.  
It depends on just one thing: **being a human, alive in time**.

That’s a major break from how money usually works.

In fiat systems, most new money is created by banks and states, typically through debt.  
In Bitcoin, the system is open and decentralized, but money creation strongly favors early adopters and machines.  
In Ğ1, money is created **directly and equally by people**, day after day.

An important clarification: Ğ1 does not try to control prices or enforce scarcity.  
Its goal is much simpler — **fair money creation between people and between generations**.

# The Universal Dividend (How Money Is Created)

The heart of Ğ1 is the **Universal Dividend (UD)**.

Every certified member of the network receives the same amount of newly created money on a regular basis. This amount is calculated from the average money per person in the system, so it naturally adapts over time.

What this creates is fairness:

* between people alive today
* and between people born earlier or later

At current levels, a certified member receives roughly:

* about 10 Ğ1 per day
* around 300 Ğ1 per month
* roughly 3,650 Ğ1 per year

This money appears automatically in your wallet. You don’t apply. You don’t owe anything. You don’t need permission.

It’s important to be clear about what this is **not**.  
The UD is **not a salary**, **not welfare**, and **not charity**.

It simply means:

>*You exist, therefore you participate in the economy.*

A helpful way to understand this is through time.

If Alice joins the system at 20 years old and uses it for 60 years, she will have created much more money overall than Bob, who joins at 60 and uses it for 20 years.  
But while they are both alive, Alice and Bob receive the **same share**.

The system makes people **equal in the present**, not equal forever.

# Thinking About Money Relatively

## test

The Relative Theory of Money treats money like time or distance: a **relative measuring tool**, not an absolute one.

Prices change because reference points change. Human lives are limited, so money must scale with time instead of allowing infinite power to accumulate for early holders.

Rather than thinking only in raw numbers, people often think in **economic time**. For example:

* 3,000 Ğ1 represents roughly 10 months of Universal Dividend
* 30,000 Ğ1 represents roughly 100 months

What matters is not **how much**, but **how long**.

Old money doesn’t disappear, but it gradually loses dominance — not by force, but by design.

# How It Works Technically (Without Going Too Deep)

Ğ1 runs on an open-source, peer-to-peer blockchain called **Duniter**.

Duniter takes care of block creation, member verification, and the automatic generation of the Universal Dividend. Instead of relying on governments or centralized ID systems, it uses a **Web of Trust** to prevent fake identities.

The key idea is simple:  
**fairness rules are enforced by software, not by politicians.**

Duniter is currently evolving toward a newer version (Duniter v2), with better scalability and more modular governance, but the underlying principles remain the same.

# Using Ğ1 Day to Day

Most people interact with Ğ1 through a wallet called **Cesium**, which works on mobile, web, and desktop.

There are two main types of users:

* certified members, who co-create the Universal Dividend
* simple wallets, which can send and receive Ğ1 but don’t create money

On top of that, tools like Cesium+ add profiles, classifieds, and local marketplaces.

To become a certified member, a person must be verified by at least five other real people. Certifications expire and must be renewed, which keeps the network healthy and prevents mass fake accounts.

Like open-source software, the whole system can also be forked into new currencies if communities want to experiment in different ways.

**Inflation, Prices, and the Real World**

A very common question is: **“Isn’t this inflation?”**

In Ğ1, balances do grow — but they grow **for everyone at the same pace**.

In fiat systems, inflation hurts because money is created unequally. Some people receive it first, and everyone else pays the price later.

In Ğ1, prices can move, but **relative positions remain stable**. Over time, people stop thinking mainly in terms of “how much money someone has” and start thinking in terms of “how much time this represents”.

In practice, most people still live in a mixed world. Prices are often partly in euros and partly in Ğ1. There is no official exchange rate, but shared habits emerge.

A common observation today is:

>*1 € ≈ 3–5 Universal Dividends*

So you might see things like:

* rent paid in euros
* food or services partly in Ğ1

This isn’t a contradiction — it’s simply a **transition phase**.

# Markets, Goods, and Community

Ğ1 is used in local markets for food, services, art, care, and everyday exchanges. A common saying in the community is:

>*“Economy is exchange; life is exchange.”*

These markets exist in many regions of Europe. Sometimes it’s as simple as 40 people meeting, each spending around 40 Ğ1, with about 1,600 Ğ1 circulating during the event.

There are challenges, of course. Early on, there tend to be many services and fewer physical goods, and money can accumulate in some wallets. Common responses include mixed pricing, better coordination, and gradually helping professionals join the system.

There are also paper Ğ1 notes with QR codes, mostly used for education and onboarding, to help newcomers understand that this is real money — not just numbers on a screen.

# What This Is Really About

Ğ1 is not trying to replace fiat money overnight. It doesn’t need to. The two can coexist.

At a deeper level, Ğ1 is exploring a simple question:

>*What if money creation itself were fair?*

Money is a human agreement. The Universal Dividend recognizes everyone as an economic actor — not because they work, but because they exist.

This is not a revolution.  
It’s not a promise.  
It’s a **quiet, real experiment**.

One where ethics meet economics, technology supports cooperation, and money becomes human again.
